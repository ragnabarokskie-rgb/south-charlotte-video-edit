Part one

The twelve-step edit

Each step runs in the app that does that one job best, and hands a finished file to the next. Nothing goes backwards. That is the whole point of the order. Every clip below plays on its own as you scroll past it.

Step 01

Rough cut

First pass on the raw talking head. Every retake, flub and dead take comes out here, plus the filler words that break a sentence in half. The audio is centred and normalised so the whole rest of the pipeline works from one predictable loudness.

FilmoraRaw → Cutted 1
Setting: normalise to −14 LUFS integrated, which is YouTube's own target, so nothing gets turned down on upload.

Step 02

Remove silence

The pacing pass. Not filler words, the breaths and gaps between sentences, which is what actually makes a 20-minute video feel long. Detect silence, ripple delete. What comes out the other side is the same take, just tighter: the sentences land back to back and the whole thing moves quicker without ever sounding rushed.

Premiere ProClean Cut
Tuned threshold: −38 to −40 dB. Anything shallower starts eating the front of words; anything deeper leaves the pauses in.

Step 03

Colour correction & grading

Correct first, then grade. Correction fixes what the camera got wrong: exposure, white balance, skin tone. Grading is the look on top. This happens before b-roll goes in, so the stock footage can be matched to the talking head rather than the other way round.

DaVinci ResolveGraded master
Watch out: the camera records HLG / HDR, which reads washed-out and flat until a Rec.2100 HLG → Rec.709 conversion is applied. Do that first or every later judgement is wrong.

Step 04

Download the b-roll

Play the cut back and listen to it while you work, so you can hear what each line is asking for. Then go to Envato or Google and find the shot that actually suits that moment. Over-collect on purpose: it is far cheaper to have a spare shot than to break flow later looking for one.

EnvatoGoogle
Naming rule: every file goes in as MM.SS_description.mov so it drops onto the timeline at the right moment.

Step 06

Lay in b-roll & effects

B-roll at its timecode, the alpha motion graphics on top, transitions and sound effects in between. Speed over neatness here. The goal is a timeline that plays start to finish, not a pretty one.

Order matters: the AE cards export as QuickTime with alpha, so they sit above the b-roll without a matte.

Step 07

Refine, clip by clip

Now the slow pass. Walk every single clip: is the b-roll on the right word, is the scale and position right, does the transition land on the beat, is anything sitting there with no effect on it. Write down the shots that are still missing and go get them.

Premiere ProSlowest step
Rule: if a b-roll shot does not say something the words are not already saying, cut it. Coverage is not the goal.

Step 08

Music & ducking

Licensed music from Envato, laid under the whole cut with the licence saved next to the project file. The bed ducks under the voice automatically so it lifts in the gaps and disappears under speech, never fighting the narration.

EnvatoPremiere ProLicence archived
Keep the receipt. Save the licence PDF in the project folder. That file is the only defence against a music claim later.

Step 09

Full rewatch, then export

One uninterrupted watch of the whole thing at normal speed, as a viewer, not as an editor. No scrubbing, no fixing on the fly. Note the timecodes, fix them in one batch, then export. This is the pass that catches the mistake everyone else would have spotted first.

Premiere ProFinal master
Check the ends. First 30 seconds and last 30 seconds get watched twice. That is the hook and the call to action.

Why this one broke out

It was news, not a guide

Most of the channel is evergreen advice, which competes with three years of its own back catalogue. This one had a date on it: toll lanes opened 28 February 2026, a revaluation, a school capacity vote. Nobody else had covered that week.

Numbers instead of adjectives

$1B, $490M, 18 miles, +59%, 2030. Every claim is a figure with a source, which is also exactly what the motion graphics were built to display. The visuals had something concrete to animate.

Five searches in one title

Ballantyne, Waxhaw, Weddington, South Charlotte, 2026. Four town-name queries land on one video instead of being split across four thinner uploads.

A stake, not a summary

“One town just told parents there may not be room for their kids until 2030” is a consequence, not a fact. That line is doing the work in the title, the description and the cold open.

The repeatable part

Dated news + hard numbers + a multi-town title

The edit quality is what kept people watching for 20 minutes, but it is not what got them to click. The pattern worth copying is the topic shape: something that changed recently, with figures attached, covering several searchable places at once. Run the twelve steps on top of that shape and the production value compounds instead of being spent on a video nobody found.

Part three

The motion system

The graphics are not designed per video. They run off a fixed spec, so the next video inherits the same look instead of being restyled from scratch. This is that spec.

Palette

Ink#0A0A0A
Paper#FFFFFF
Bone#F4F1EC
Red · watch-out#FF2D2D
Volt · upside#D7FF1E
Fog#6B6B6B
Steel#8A8A8A
Hair#C7C7C7

Colour carries meaning, not decoration. Volt means opportunity, red means watch out, ink means neutral editorial. A viewer learns the code in the first two cards and reads every later card faster because of it.

Build spec

  • Comp size / frame rate1920×1080 · 30fps
  • Display faceAnton, tracking −0.03em
  • Label faceJetBrains Mono Bold, +0.16em
  • Headline auto-fit width1560px max
  • Base display size138px, shrinks to fit
  • Card duration4.2 to 4.6s
  • Gap between cards0.35s hold
  • Layer namingNN_MM.SS_slug__element
  • Chapter cards exportQuickTime + alpha
  • Number cards exportH.264, 15 Mbps
11

Chapter cards

White stage, ink type. One per narration section, from the $1B Ballantyne open to the closing call to action. Tag chip slams in from the left, headline lines stagger, rule grows from centre, kicker rises last.

9

Intro beats

Black stage. The cold open lives on black so the cut to the talking head lands. Hook, three stat slams, the school line, the five pockets list, the triad, then the title card at 1:06.

13

Number cards

Counting figures for every dollar amount and tax rate in the script. Volt for investment, red for your tax bill, ink for market price, the same colour code as the chapter cards.

The four easing curves

  • Slam · quart in-out. Chips, headlines, wipes.impact
  • Snap · exponential out. Opacity, rises.arrival
  • Glide · cosine. Slow drifts.settle
  • Linear · counters and bars.neutral

Everything on screen uses one of these four. That constraint is why 33 separately-built cards feel like one title sequence rather than a folder of graphics.

Two rules that save the render

Background twice over

Set comp.bgColor and add a real locked solid at the bottom of the stack. The comp colour alone does not render into an alpha export. The solid is what actually shows up.

Name every layer

NN_MM.SS_slug__element. The timecode is in the layer name, so a card can be found, re-rendered and dropped back into Premiere at the right frame.

Part four

Thumbnail anatomy

Three colours, three lines, one face, one landmark. Every element earns its place by being readable at 210 pixels wide, because that is the only size that actually decides whether the video gets watched.

Seven things it does right

1

Three lines, three weights

SOUTH in white, CHARLOTTE in gold, JUST CHANGED! reversed out of red. The eye reads them in that order and gets the whole promise in under a second, with no sentence to parse.

2

Gold is the loudest word

Warm gold against a cool blue sky is the strongest contrast pair on the plate, so the place name is what survives at feed size. The place name is also the search term.

3

Red only for the verb

One red element, used once, on the words that carry the urgency. A second red element would cancel the first. The discipline is what makes it read as urgent.

4

Heavy black stroke and drop shadow

Every letter is separated from the photo underneath. Without it the white line dies against the bright cloud and the gold dies against the sunset.

5

Face right, type left

Subject cut out and pushed to the right third, eyes to camera, genuine open smile. Type owns the left half. Nothing overlaps, so nothing competes.

6

A landmark doing double duty

The Ballantyne tower and the light-trail curve of I-485 are literally the two subjects of the video. The background is not decoration. It is the second and third headline.

7

The 485 shield

One small badge in the lower left. Locals recognise it instantly; it signals “this is about your commute” without spending a single word of headline on it.

The reusable formula: [PLACE in white] · [PLACE in gold] · [WHAT CHANGED! reversed out of red] · face right · landmark behind · one local icon. Swap the place and the verb; keep everything else.
Part five

Run it again

Turning one good result into a system means writing down what to repeat. This is the working checklist for the next video in this series.

Before the camera turns on

  • Pick a topic with a date attached, something that changed in the last 90 days.
  • Collect at least seven hard numbers with sources. These become the number cards.
  • Name three or more searchable places that can share one title.
  • Write the cold open around a consequence, not a summary.

During the edit

  • Rough cut to −14 LUFS before anything else touches the audio.
  • Silence pass at −38 to −40 dB before the b-roll goes anywhere near the timeline.
  • Keep the motion graphics on the house spec; never restyle a one-off card.
  • Rewatch the whole cut once, straight through, before exporting.

After the upload

  • Chapters every 60 to 120 seconds, named with the payoff, not the topic.
  • Lead magnet above the description fold.
  • Thumbnail checked at 210px wide before it goes live.
  • Blog post from the transcript within the first week.
  • Log views at day 7 and day 30 against the channel median. That is the only honest scoreboard.
The whole thing in one line

Cut for pace, grade before b-roll, script the graphics, package like it matters.

Twelve steps, five apps, one direction of travel. Every step hands a finished file to the next one and never asks for it back, which is why a 20-minute video carrying this much b-roll and motion graphics is a repeatable week of work instead of a one-off.
